## Macro view

Last week was the worst for US equities since April 2025: the Dow fell 8%, the S&P 500 8.6% and the Nasdaq 10% as the AI trade sold off (W1, PKG). The macro backdrop is hawkish. CPI inflation is running at 4.1% y/y, the 10-year yield is about 5.0% and the fed funds upper bound is 4.0% (PKG). Prediction markets price a roughly 90% chance of a 25bp hike on Dec 9 (W3, W4). Goldman's panic index is near its maximum. Its traders warn the selling could continue, but they also see an entry point (W10, W11). Coming into a short Thanksgiving week, I lean modestly contrarian toward cheap, heavily sold large caps without company-specific scandals. Crowded, expensive defensives that held up best look weaker relative bets if the market rebounds. High rates still weigh on housing-linked names. Policy risk (Medicare for All talk, wider Medicare Advantage audits) hangs over managed care (W7).

- Themes: market_sentiment, fed_and_rates, inflation
- Favoured sectors: Communication Services, Financials, Industrials, Information Technology
- Disfavoured sectors: Consumer Staples, Consumer Discretionary (housing/restaurants), Managed Care

## Picks rationale

Positioning is stretched after the worst week since April 2025, and Goldman's panic index reached 9.8 out of 10. In such conditions, recent heavy losers that are cheap often bounce over short periods. GOOGL (13.9x earnings), GS (11.4x) and CAT (20x, up after hours on Friday) were sold hard without company scandals. CRM's selloff was sector-wide and it reports on Dec 2. I avoided NVDA despite its larger drop because the SEC probe and CEO turmoil add risk specific to the company. The main risk: the selling continues and defensives keep leading.
